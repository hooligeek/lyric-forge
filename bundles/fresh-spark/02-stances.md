# The stance roster

A song's **stance** is how it is spoken, independent of what it is about.
This is the axis that vocabulary lists cannot constrain, and it is where
sameness accumulates: a catalogue can rotate its topics for years while
every song remains the same speech act.

**Rule: a stance may not repeat until at least two others have been used.**

## Accusation  (`accusation`)

Second person, adversarial. The listener is the opponent and is told what they are. The roster's default and by far its most overused mode.

*Syntactic marker:* you + present-tense indictment

*Example:* "Calling it lazy 'cause you lost the fight"

*Caution:* Nine of the twenty-one existing tracks are this. Treat it as spent until the distribution evens out.

## Exhortation  (`exhortation`)

Second person, allied. The listener is a comrade being urged, not an enemy being indicted. Same grammatical person as accusation, opposite temperature.

*Syntactic marker:* imperative + warmth

*Example:* "So artists hold your ground, don't turn away in dread"

## Testimony  (`testimony`)

First person declaring outward what the speaker is. Not admitting, not apologising — stating. The only stance permitted to voice the machine.

*Syntactic marker:* I am / I do, addressed to a listener

*Example:* "I am just a helper in the architecture grand"

## Confession  (`confession`)

First person admitting cost, weakness, complicity, or defeat. The stance the roster most conspicuously lacks, and where every "cannot admit" line in the substrate belongs.

*Syntactic marker:* I + the thing it costs

*Example:* "While I'm deciding if I should sell my boat"

## Narrative  (`narrative`)

Third person. A specific character does specific things in a specific place. No addressee at all. Forces scene-writing instead of position-taking.

*Syntactic marker:* he/she/they + a scene

*Example:* "I'm scrolling through the feed, a barista taking aim"

## Reportage  (`reportage`)

Flat facts, stacked, no opinion offered and no one addressed. The argument is made entirely by selection. Extremely hard and extremely effective.

*Syntactic marker:* declarative sentences, no second person, no adjectives of judgement

## Dialogue  (`dialogue`)

Quoted or overheard speech, ideally the opponent's own words verbatim. The Live Fire comment harvest is a corpus purpose-built for this and has been used for exactly one song.

*Syntactic marker:* quotation marks doing the structural work

## Procedure  (`procedure`)

Instructions. Steps in order. A manual, a checklist, a sequence of commands. Cold, sequential, and unnervingly effective at conveying strain because it never mentions the strain.

*Syntactic marker:* numbered or sequential imperatives, no emotional vocabulary

*Example:* "Throw the breaker. Seal the perimeter. Compile."

## Inventory  (`inventory`)

Enumeration. A list of objects, costs, errors, parts, or names. Accretion does the emotional work; the list is never commented on.

*Syntactic marker:* nouns in series

## Retrospective  (`retrospective`)

Spoken from after it is over, looking back. Reframes present urgency as something already survived or already lost.

*Syntactic marker:* past tense + a vantage point later than the events

## Apostrophe  (`apostrophe`)

Direct address to a thing that cannot answer — the GPU, the case fan, the reed, the router, the dead drive. Turns the hardware from scenery into a character.

*Syntactic marker:* second person aimed at an object
